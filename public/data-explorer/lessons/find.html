What this lesson is for

Until now you have looked at your whole table. But usually you want just a part of it — only your landing site, only the big catches, or only a few columns.

This lesson gives you three simple commands to do that: list the values in a column, keep only the rows you want, and keep only the columns you want. Press Run on each box. Remember: you cannot break anything.

These commands come from a free toolkit called dplyr. Run the box below once to switch it on (the first time, it may take a few moments to download) — it also loads the small example table from the last lesson.

Show only the rows you want

filter() keeps only the rows that match a condition you give — for example, only the gillnet trips.

Two small things to get right:

  • Use two equals signs == for “is equal to” (one = means something else and will not work).
  • A word like "gillnet" needs quotes. A number, like 5, does not.

Press Run:

site gear fish catch_kg
Shimoni hand line Tilapia 12
Shimoni hand line Snapper 5
Shimoni hand line Octopus 3
Vanga gillnet Tilapia 8
Vanga gillnet Snapper 6

filter(gear == "gillnet") keeps the green rows and drops the rest.

Do two steps at once: the pipe |>

Most real questions need two steps: keep only your site, and keep only a few columns. The little symbol |> (called the pipe) lets you do steps in order, left to right. Read it as the word “then”.

The box below says: take the landings, then keep only Vanga, then keep these three columns. Press Run:

This is the kind of question you will ask most often: narrow the rows, then narrow the columns. Change "Vanga" to one of your own sites and press Run again.

What you have learned

  • distinct(table, column) — list the values in a column once (find your sites, gears, …).
  • filter(table, condition) — keep only the rows that match (use ==, and quotes for words).
  • select(table, columns…) — keep only the columns you name.
  • |> (“then”) — do several steps in order.
